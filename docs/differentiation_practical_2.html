8  Gene regulation in space

Auxin regulates virtually all aspects of plant development

In this practical you will study the effects of auxin, a plant hormone, on gene regulation in the root. Auxin is a hormone that is virtually involved in all developmental processes in plants, from how the plant embryo is patterned, how the organs in flowers are determined, the spatial disposition of leaves, among many many others. In each context, auxin triggers very specific responses, posing the question of how is it that the same molecule can trigger so many different responses. This specificity can be explained by the underlying regulatory networks.

The auxin signalling pathway activates transcription factors, the auxin response factors (ARFs), which regulate gene expression in response to auxin. Arabidopsis has 23 genes coding for ARFs, some regulate gene expression positively and others negatively, they can also form protein complexes with other transcription factors, and important for this practical: they are expressed in different tissues. It is this diversity in transcription factors activity and expression, which in part explains how auxin regulates so many different aspects of plants

Auxin activates and represses the expression of WOX5 in the root

Plant root growth is driven by the activity of stem cells housed in a niche at the root tip. These stem cells self-renew and produce new cells, acting as a “growth engine”. A subset of these cells express WOX5; these cells are really important as they maintain the stem cell niche. WOX5 is regulated by several factors, and network models help us understand how. Researchers have found paradoxical results regarding how auxin regulates WOX5: some experiments show that auxin promotes its expression, while others found it is repressed (Figure 1).

In the previous practical we studied a Boolean network model of the root, in which Auxin does this opposite regulation through different ARFs (Figure 2). Here we are going to use this model in an in silico root model to explain the strange experimental results in Figure 1.

The network model

In the Boolean model WOX5 is regulated positively by ARF5, negatively by ARF10, and negatively also by CLE40. Both ARF5 and ARF10 are auxin response factors, activated by auxin.

\(WOX5_{t+1} =NOT\) \(ARF10\) \(AND\) \(ARF5\) \(AND\) \(NOT\) \(CLE40\)


The expression of ARF5 and ARF10 is regulated by other transcription factors, causing them to be expressed in specific cells of the root as described by the attractors recovered by the model (see Figure 3).

Notice how the attractors differ in their expression of ARF10 and ARF5, and how the QC attractor is the only with the following combination: ARF5 active, ARF10 inactive, CLE40 inactive; therefore allowing for WOX5 expression

Here you will explore whether this is relevant to understanding the auxin–WOX5 experimental paradox. For this you will use an in silico root model in which each cell carries a copy of the Boolean network initialized in a particular attractor

The root model

Today you will use a multicellular model that simulates the cells of a root tip (Figure 3, right). To build this model we use classes. We create a class Cell, and a class Tissue that is made up of Cells. The Cells are arranged in layers to model the 8 tissue layers of the root, and initialized in the attractor corresponding to their position.The model simulates the transport of auxin from cell to cell, where their cell type defines the direction of polar auxin transport.

In figure 3 (right) you can see the output of the model showing the distribution of auxin in the middle cell layer, and different properties of `Cells``: auxin levels (log), the cell types (attractor initialized), and gene expression (in the figure we see only ARF10, ARF5, MGP and WOX5).

Files

Download the python files from https://github.com/moneralee/UU_Modeling-Life-course-2026/tree/main/WC08-Cell-differentiation_II.
You will find:

WC08Cell.py

The class Cell is defined here.

Attributes: A Cell position is defined by ypos and layer; auxin stores the current auxin level and auxinhelper is used for the transport simulation; cell-type is predefined based on their position (1-QC, 2-Columella, 3-Endodermis, 4-Vascular, 5-TD, 6-Epidermis, 7-Cortex); cell_state is an array with the expression of each node of the network, which are modelled as individual attributes: CK, ARR1, etc.

Methods: __init__ is used to initialize the cells; root_ode defines the ODE network (you will need to change this during the practical); update_network_state is used to solve the network; update_cell_state that updates the value of the genes (Cell’s attributes) based on the output of update_network_state.

WC08Tissue.py

The class Tissue is defined here.

Attributes: A Tissue is composed of 50 cells in each layer (8).

Methods: load_state reads a file with an initial condition (initial_tissue_state.json) with cells arranged in cell files and rows, and their attractors are predefined based on how these cell types are organized in a root. add_cell adds cells objects to tissue. get_cell finds the cell in a given position in the matrix (useful to find the immediate neighbors of a cell). update_networks updates the networks in each cell of the tissue (by calling the method cell.update_network_state). Auxin_Passive_Transport simulates the passive diffusion of auxin between neighboring cells (using the get_cell function). Auxin_Active_Transport simulates the active transport of auxin taking into account their cell type that determines the direction of the transport; This is a very large function because it checks the cell type of each cell in a cell layer. Auxin_Source_Decay auxin enters the root from the upper central cell, and decays in all cells. visualize creates the plot of the tissue.

WC08_Root-tissue-model.py

The main file of the model. It defines the parameters, and the simulate_development function that uses the methods from the Tissue and Cell classes to 1) update the cell networks using tissue.update_networks, 2) simulate active auxin transport using Auxin_Active_transport, 3) simulate passive auxin transport using Auxin_Passive_Transport, 4) update auxin input and decay with tissue.Auxin_Source_Decay, 5) and usestissue.visualize for the output plot of the model. Run this file, and if the plotting updates too slow increase the value in plt.pause(1).

initial_tissue_state.json

This is the initial condition of the model defining how cells are organized in a tissue, their attractors, and the auxin distribution.

8.1 Questions

Exercise 8.1 (Algorithmic thinking) Study how the model uses the different functions to simulate gene regulation and auxin transport in the root.

Make a plain-language description, pseudocode, or flowchart using pen and paper of how the model works. What are the algorithmic steps you would take if you want to simulate the treatment of roots with auxin?

Exercise 8.2 (Biology) What happens if you update the network more often than the auxin transport? How would you implement this modification in the code?

Exercise 8.3 (Biology & algorithmic thinking) Now let’s use the model to simulate the auxin treatments from the experiments in Figure 1. To simulate an auxin treatment change the value of \(AuxinTreatment\) in Root-model-Auxin.py. Try values of 10 and 750. This increases auxin levels in all cells by the same amount. You can run one treatment at a time, or make a for loop and run the three in one go.

  • How does auxin treatment affect gene expression in the root?
  • Does the model output correspond to the transcriptional reporter of WOX5 shown in Figure 1?
  • Do you see activation or repression as reported experimentally? (You can visualize different genes by changing the grid provided to plotGrids(), although the labelling will be wrong.)

Notice the simulation creates an output folder with png images.

Exercise 8.4 (Biology & algorithmic/mathematical thinking) The model does not yet reproduce the experiments. Yet, we can use this model to test hypotheses and aim to recover such behaviours (Figure 1). Let’s implement the following two hypotheses:

Modify the equations for MGP and WOX5 in rootNetwork accordingly.

  • For MGP, retain its logical rule but multiply its production term by a negative regulatory term of auxin (\(Km = 45\)): \(\frac{45}{45+auxininput}\)

  • Similarly, add a negative regulation by auxin to the WOX5 equation (\(Km = 1000\)).

Run control and auxin treatments with (i) updated MGP, (ii) updated WOX5, and (iii) both updates. Use the plotting functions to plot the final timepoint.

It is also interesting to run the timecourse, by plotting every 10 steps.

Do you now see the differences reported by experimentalists?

Exercise 8.5 (Biological interpretation) You should observe that WOX5 expression gradually expands into the endodermis and disappears from QC cells when both equations are updated with the two hypotheses. This matches the experimental microscopy from Figure 1.

  • Why do different parts of the root respond differently to auxin?
  • Why does auxin regulation of MGP do? (Look at the network…)
  • Why does induction of WOX5 occur mainly in “upper” cells and repression in the cells at the bottom?

Exercise 8.6 (Algorithmic thinking) Experimental repression of WOX5 was detected by RT-PCR at the very tip (Figure 1, right panel). Reproduce this in silico by saving the average WOX5 levels of cells at the tip (\(y\) < 40) at the end of the simulation for all three treatments. For comparison, also calculate the average for the whole root. Then plot this average WOX5 expression levels (y axis) for each auxinTreatment= 0, 10 and 750 (x axis).

Tip: WOX5Grid stores WOX5 levels, and \(y\) controls the position of the cells with \(y=0\) being the bottom of the root.

  • What is the overall effect of auxin treatments in the root tip? Considering that the root is a mixture of many cell types, which cells best represent the plotted values?

Exercise 8.7 (Biology & algorithmic thinking) Now, let’s compare WOX5 regulation across tissues. Let’s use the model to do an in RT-PCR in silico analysis of only the cells in the QC position, and also the endodermis cells. This has not been done experimentally and thus you will generate a prediction (!).

Tip: cell types are stored in cellGrid.

  • Compare what you see in the root simulation, and what you obtain in the simulated RT-PCR plot. Which case represents better how WOX5 is regulated in the root tip? Why?

Exercise 8.8 (Biology & algorithmic thinking) Changes in WOX5 expression depend on tissue context and auxin dosage. Simulate a range of auxin treatments from 0 to 750.

Can you now explain the auxin–WOX5 paradox? What mechanism do you propose?

Exercise 8.9 (Biology & conceptual thinking)  

  • Perform an in silico intervention to alter WOX5 dosage responsiveness to auxin. For example, what would it take to enforce its continuous expression, or block completely its induction in the endodermis.

  • How would you test these model predictions experimentally?

Exercise 8.10 (Biological thinking) All models are wrong, but some are useful. The model we studied today offered us a mechanistic insight into the paradoxical auxin responses. Yet, (to make things more fun), the original experiments used different auxin analogs and other molecules which are transported very differently between cells: NAA (transported independently of PINs), IAA (PIN-transported), and NPA (blocks PINs, increasing auxin in the tip). How does this information change your conclusions? What extensions to the model would you make to account for these differences?
